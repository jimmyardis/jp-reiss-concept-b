03 / Testing guide

The tests, and what each one tells you.

These sampling methods may be recommended based on inspection findings and environmental conditions. The type and number of samples are determined during the assessment.

Air sampling

Used selectively. Samples are collected from within wall, floor or ceiling cavities and other isolated spaces to detect hidden mold potentially caused by moisture. A moment-in-time snapshot that may not always confirm abnormal conditions.

Results: up to 1 week

Surface sampling

Swab samples are collected from visible areas of suspected mold growth to confirm contamination sources in specific locations.

Results: up to 1 week

ERMI

Analyzes settled dust from living areas, contents and ventilation systems to identify and quantify specific mold species. Ideal for detecting cross-contamination and water-damage-related molds, especially where immunocompromised people live.

Results: 1–2 weeks

Mycotoxin sampling

Detects five groups of toxic compounds produced by molds: trichothecenes, gliotoxins, aflatoxins, ochratoxin A and zearalenone. These can severely impact health, especially in sensitive populations.

Results: up to 2 weeks

Endotoxin sampling

Identifies toxic components from the outer membranes of Gram-negative bacteria, often inhaled or ingested. Health effects vary with dosage and route of exposure.

Results: up to 2 weeks

Actinobacteria sampling

Targets actinomycetes, bacteria commonly found in water-damaged buildings. A scoring system compares levels to those typically found in non-damaged structures.

Results: up to 2 weeks

Sampling only proceeds with your full understanding and approval, so our recommendations stay aligned with your goals and concerns.

04 / About

Joseph P. Reiss, Jr., Indoor Environmental Professional.

Joe has more than 30 years of hands-on experience across construction, indoor environmental assessment and remediation, and indoor air quality. He built his assessment program for the difficult cases: homes where something is wrong and no one has been able to say what.

His mission is to uncover root causes and provide practical, lasting solutions that improve indoor air quality and quality of life. Through JP Reiss Jr. Environmental Group he serves clients across the East Coast and beyond, and he co-authors published research on how mold and environmental toxins affect health.
